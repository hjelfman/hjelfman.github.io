# the author experienced a terrible illness after this post

First, you have to wake up the morning after Yom Kippur stuffed on bagels and salad pizza—really, a bit uncomfortably full, slogging through twelve miles as it feels like your intestines are being scraped with a spoon. Unfortunately, the rest of the week serves to remind you that you're in a body subject to intense abdominal pain. It isn't until the next Monday that you start to feel yourself again. However, just as you're recovering, you have to go back to Dallas for a wedding; though the plentiful food helps, the deviation from your usual rice and Huel diet invites quite a bit more pain and a worse flare-up as you spike a fever on the plane ride home. The Texan miles weren't helping as Garland's sidewalks are vestigial, serving more as traps for aspiring pedestrians than a means of conveyance. This is hard on your ankles and heels; in the last mile of your Texas run you feel a pop and stiffness in your ankle. On the plane ride back, desperate for cures for these problems, you order peptides purported to cure some of this.

With the better pavement of the West Side Highway, Monday and Tuesday make for pleasant runs until your knee gives way Wednesday, such that keeping it in any static position for a length of time makes it difficult to move. Despite this, you run until Friday, on which you compound and inject the peptides you ordered. It is not a magic cure. However, your ankle, which has bothered you for years now, hurts less day over day. And a few stretches before running reduce the knee pain. Rinse and repeat: ankle pain lessening by the day as you run at 6am, or 5am some days, to ensure that you can fit a run in. While the West Side Highway is the most pleasant route while the sun is rising, six miles down Broadway is a far easier run on days you are particularly wiped. On weekends, long runs are quite nice, going north to Yonkers, around the southern tip of Manhattan, or over the RFK Bridge through Astoria & Williamsburg. On rainier days, the treadmill at Planet Fitness makes for a boring minimum run. All of these are much more enjoyable without ankle braces.

Distance running is an exercise in time management; it is difficult to keep a loose schedule with 90 waking minutes dedicated to exercise. Some may like to supplement this time with an audiobook or podcast (in all fairness, I listen to *Well There's Your Problem* on my indoor runs). I much prefer to keep my time open during these blocks, a time for only thought and exercise. The accumulation of miles becomes meditative after a point, a time to exist with only thought until those too run out. 

After a month you've achieved your streak. You're also out of thoughts for a while.
